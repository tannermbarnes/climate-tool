Estimate how a sealed section of a mine or cave exchanges heat, then ask how cold ventilation could make it. Measure the room's thermal mass (Croom) and conductance to the rock (UA) from a simple fan trial, then drive the model with real hourly weather for a whole winter.

How it works

Steps 1 and 4 work on their own with manual values; steps 2 and 3 use a fan trial.
01

Steady state

From an assumed U and A, tabulate the temperature the room settles at for a range of outside temperatures and airflows.

02

Fan experiment

Paste logger data from a fan trial. Fit the cooling (Initial) and recovery (Return) slopes to estimate Croom and UA.

03

Local simulation

Check the fitted values by simulating a short run at a fixed outside temperature and airflow.

04

Season

Pull hourly weather for any location and date range and simulate the internal temperature across a winter.

Method

A single well-mixed air volume exchanging heat with ventilation air and with the surrounding rock.

Ventilation heat capacity rate

ṁcp = ρ Q cp

Q is airflow (m³/s; 1 CFM = 4.719 × 10⁻⁴ m³/s), ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, cp = 1005 J/kg·K.

Steady state (steps 1, 3, 4)

Tss = (ṁcp Ta + UA Tr) / (ṁcp + UA)

The room settles between the outside air temperature Ta and the rock temperature Tr, weighted by airflow and conductance.

Thermal mass from the Initial window

Croom = | ṁcp · mean(Text − Tint) / (dTint/dt) |

The slope is a linear fit to Internal Avg over the selected points; the temperature difference is averaged over the same points.

Conductance from the Return window

UA = Croom · |dTint/dt| / |Tnow − Tinit|

Tinit is Internal Avg at the start of the Initial window; Tnow is Internal Avg at the start of the Return window.

Time course (steps 3 and 4)

T(t) = Tss + (T0 − Tss) · exp(−(ṁcp + UA) t / Croom)

Step 4 applies this hour by hour, recomputing Tss from each hour's outside temperature. The 2–6 °C band reported in step 4 is a summary target, not a model output.
